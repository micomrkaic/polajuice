What do these cameras and films do?

Cameras

The camera is the format and process: optics, grain, flash, framing. Traits below are generated from the engine's own parameters, so they always match what actually renders.

Films

The film is the color chemistry, applied as a 3D LUT. "Canonical (auto)" picks each camera's period-correct stock; Age is a third axis — storage fog, faded contrast and a magenta drift that works with any camera and film. Developing is the chemistry: push for contrast and grain, pull to soften, or cross-process slide film for shifted colors — disabled for instant cameras, which develop inside the film unit. Grain model chooses where the grain comes from: classic lays display noise over the finished picture; silver forms it from exposure inside the film, so shadows run coarse and highlights fine, and print stock, push and age act on it as on the image. Edge effects are the developer exhausting locally — the dense side of a boundary develops denser and small dense details most (border, fringe and Eberhard effects) — 1 is a dilute developer, 2 stand development; uniform areas are untouched. Raw files (.nef, .dng) come through a scene-linear door: the sensor's white sits a stop above display white and the top stop rolls in through a shoulder, so halation and the film curve see real highlights instead of a plateau; Exposure moves from there. JPEG and PNG render exactly as before. Halation scales the camera's glow, strongest from raw.
